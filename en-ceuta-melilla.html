TWO CITIES, MULTIPLE DIMENSIONS

Ceuta and Melilla: sovereignty, border and strategic value

The two Spanish cities in North Africa are external EU borders, port nodes, military spaces and central symbols in relations with Morocco.

LEGAL AND POLITICAL FACT

Spain exercises sovereignty; Morocco claims the cities

Spain regards both cities as integral parts of its territory. Morocco maintains a longstanding claim. The existence of that political dispute does not itself prove an immediate operation to alter sovereignty.

Key point

Statements about future intentions must be separated from known official positions.

01

European border

They are the EU's only territories with a direct land border with Africa and operate under specific border and Schengen controls.

02

Ports and ferries

They connect people, vehicles, goods and services with mainland Spain and the Moroccan hinterland.

03

Security

Police and military presence, fences and breakwaters make every crisis a national matter.

04

Bilateral symbol

Sovereignty, customs, migration and cross-border commerce reappear during diplomatic tensions.

THE DIFFICULT QUESTION

Does Morocco seek control of the Strait through Ceuta and Melilla?

FACTS
  • Morocco claims both cities.
  • It has material influence over border and migration cooperation.
  • Tanger Med has become a major port competitor.
  • Spain and Morocco cooperate intensively on trade and security.
PLAUSIBLE INTERPRETATION
  • Greater influence over the cities would strengthen Morocco's position around the Strait.
  • Border crises can create diplomatic leverage without an immediate territorial goal.
  • Port and logistics competition increases the corridor's value.
NOT PROVEN
  • That an operational plan currently exists to seize the cities.
  • That the 2026 crisis aimed to obtain maritime control.
  • That sovereignty over the cities would grant legal control of international passage.
